An insurer processes property-damage claims through a multi-stage adjudication pipeline. When a claim is submitted, an intake clerk registers it and verifies that the required documents and photos are attached. If something is missing, the claim is returned to the policyholder for completion and is re-checked once the policyholder responds, and this return-and-recheck repeats until the submission is complete.

Once the submission is complete, the insurer runs two checks concurrently: an automated engine screens the claim for fraud anomalies while, in parallel, a claims adjuster independently estimates the repair cost from the evidence. After both the fraud score and the cost estimate are available, a routing decision consolidates them. A low-value, low-risk claim is approved automatically. A high-value but low-risk claim is sent to a senior adjuster for manual review, who either approves it or rejects it. A claim the engine flags as high-risk is escalated to the special-investigations unit, which gathers additional evidence and then either clears it for approval or denies it for fraud.

Whenever a claim is denied, the policyholder is notified and may file an appeal. An appealed claim is re-examined by an independent reviewer who either overturns the denial and approves the claim or upholds the denial; if the denial is upheld, the policyholder may appeal again, and this appeal cycle continues until the claim is either approved or the policyholder accepts the final decision.

When a claim is approved, the insurer calculates the settlement amount and, only if the loss was caused by an identifiable third party, additionally opens a subrogation case to recover the costs from that party; otherwise this step is skipped. The payment is then issued to the policyholder.

Regardless of the outcome -- automatic approval, approval after review, payout after appeal, or a final denial -- the insurer records the decision and its rationale in the claims ledger, closes the file, and archives the supporting documents for the retention period.
