When a patient arrives at the emergency department, a triage nurse registers the patient and records the vital signs and the presenting complaint. Based on an acuity assessment, the patient is routed onto one of three tracks: an immediately life-threatening case goes straight to the resuscitation bay, an urgent case is placed in the main treatment area, and a minor case is directed to the fast-track clinic. If no bed on the assigned track is free, the patient waits and is re-assessed at fixed intervals, and keeps waiting and being re-assessed until an appropriate bed becomes available.

Once the patient occupies a treatment space, an emergency physician performs an initial examination and orders a diagnostic work-up. Blood samples are sent to the laboratory and, at the same time, the required imaging is performed in the radiology suite. The physician waits until both the laboratory results and the imaging report are available and then consolidates them into a working diagnosis.

Depending on the working diagnosis, the physician either starts treatment directly or, when the case lies outside the physician's scope, first requests a specialist consultation; in that case the consultant reviews the patient and agrees a plan, and only then does treatment begin. The treatment is delivered and the patient's response is monitored. If the patient has not stabilized, the plan is adjusted and delivered again and the response is monitored once more, and this adjust-and-monitor cycle repeats until the patient is finally stable.

When the patient is stable, the physician decides on a disposition and the patient is either discharged home with instructions, admitted to an inpatient ward, or transferred to another facility offering a higher level of care. Separately, at any time after registration the patient may decide to leave against medical advice, which ends the visit early once the risks have been documented.

In every case -- discharge, admission, transfer, or leaving against advice -- the department finalizes the medical record, updates the bed-management system, and sends a summary to the patient's primary-care provider before the encounter is closed.
